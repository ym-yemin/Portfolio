Myanmar's energy system combines traditional biomass, hydropower, natural gas, petroleum products and a growing interest in renewable-energy technologies.

Rising electricity demand and the need to expand energy access have made the development of the country's energy infrastructure an important long-term challenge.

## Energy access

Historically, electricity consumption per person has remained low compared with many neighbouring countries. Expanding the national grid, developing mini-grids and deploying standalone systems have all been considered pathways for improving rural electrification.

## Solar potential

Myanmar has substantial solar resources. Solar mini-grids and solar home systems can be particularly useful in locations where extending the national electricity grid is expensive or technically difficult.

## Wind and other renewable resources

Wind resources also exist, especially in coastal and elevated areas. However, theoretical resource potential does not always translate directly into economically developable projects.

Grid availability, terrain, transport infrastructure and financing conditions can significantly limit what can actually be developed.

Agricultural residues and other forms of biomass may also provide energy opportunities, particularly in rural regions, provided that resources are managed sustainably.

## Policy and investment challenges

Renewable-energy potential alone is not sufficient to create a successful energy transition. Investors need predictable regulations, technical standards, transparent project procedures and access to financing.

Strengthening institutions and creating a stable policy framework are therefore essential to attracting long-term private-sector investment.

## The opportunity

Myanmar has significant renewable-energy resources and a clear need for expanded electricity access. With careful planning, improved regulation and appropriate infrastructure, renewable technologies can play a larger role in the country's future energy mix.
