Increasing the share of wind and solar power requires a transformation of the electricity system. Renewable generation is variable, so maintaining the balance between electricity supply and demand requires greater flexibility.

Dispatchable generation, energy storage, stronger networks and demand-side management can all provide this flexibility. Increasingly, these resources are connected through digital technologies.

## From electricity grid to energy internet

Sensors, software, smart meters and communication networks allow enormous amounts of information to be collected and processed throughout the power system.

This concept is sometimes described as the Internet of Energy: a digitally connected electricity system in which distributed resources can communicate and respond to system conditions.

## The rise of the prosumer

Traditional electricity systems largely separate generators from consumers. Distributed solar, batteries, electric vehicles and smart technologies can blur this boundary.

Consumers may become prosumers who both consume and produce electricity. Digital platforms could allow communities to coordinate these resources or even exchange electricity locally.

## Potential social effects

Peer-to-peer energy systems could provide people with greater control over their energy use and encourage stronger community participation in the energy system.

At the same time, energy transitions are not purely technical. Questions of access, affordability, digital inclusion, governance, privacy and community participation are equally important.

## Technology alone is not enough

Much of the discussion around digitally enabled, decentralized energy systems focuses on technical and economic performance. More attention is needed on their social consequences.

Future energy research should therefore examine not only whether these systems work technically, but also how they change relationships between communities, companies, consumers and institutions.
